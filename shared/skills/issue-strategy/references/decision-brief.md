# Decision brief

Use the parts needed for the assignment. Keep the executive view short; link
supporting evidence rather than burying the decision in a transcript.

1. **Decision needed and recommendation.** Problem, affected outcome and why now;
   one paragraph stating the recommendation and uncertainty that matters.
2. **Coverage and context.** Snapshot/date, metadata census versus deep sample,
   revisions, dirty checkout, source/telemetry coverage, known gaps and changes
   since the snapshot. A source claim is not a production observation.
3. **Evidence.** Claim, reported/verified/inferred/unknown, precise source,
   contradictory evidence and confidence. Summarize related issue clusters.
4. **Options and tradeoffs.** Status quo, minimum intervention and serious
   alternatives; impact, cost of delay, reversibility, dependencies, ownership,
   maintenance and uncertainty. State why the recommendation wins under the
   stated assumptions and what would reverse it.
5. **Recommended decisions.** For each: issue links; proposed owner role; next
   step; measure/baseline; proposed acceptance; priority rationale; revisit
   trigger. Separate immediate assessment/containment from durable remediation.
6. **Sequence.** Near-term decisions and prerequisites, followed by conditional
   investments or deferrals. Show verified and inferred dependency edges
   separately. Do not treat an issue list as an already-approved roadmap.
7. **Open questions.** Ask only questions whose answers materially alter action;
   name the evidence/person needed without fabricating an owner or commitment.

An architectural decision can be captured as a **proposed** ADR: title, status,
context, alternatives, recommendation, rationale, consequences, uncertainty,
acceptance/revisit trigger and evidence links. Only record acceptance when the
authorized decision owner actually accepts it.
